# Mechanics of Rigid and Deformable Bodies Calculator

An interactive, browser-only solver for Statics of Rigid Bodies, Dynamics of Rigid Bodies and Mechanics of Deformable Bodies, with 2D and 3D diagrams, step-by-step solutions and a printable PDF report.

American Concrete Institute Philippines T.I.P. QC Student Chapter · by CEM Jumawan

**Live site:** https://acip-tip-qc-sc.github.io/mechanics-of-bodies-calculator/

## Modules

Built so far:

- **Statics of Rigid Bodies:** Force Systems (coplanar and 3D resultants, an unknown force or angle found from the resultant required, including the smallest force and the least resultant, direction angles, components along a line, moments about a point and an axis, force-couple systems, wrenches, distributed loads); Equilibrium (particles in a plane and in space, rigid bodies in a plane and in 3D with pins, rollers, fixed supports, cables, links, ball-and-socket joints, journal and thrust bearings and hinges, with checks for statically indeterminate and improperly constrained bodies, and the largest load the supports or cables can carry; networks of cords, springs, rings and small pulleys with several joints, giving the member forces, spring stretches, unstretched lengths or stiffness, a cord in an unknown direction, the largest load for a cord capacity, or the sag at which rings settle; frames, machines and compound beams taken apart member by member, with two-force members found and the pin forces on each member); Trusses (Pratt, Howe, Warren, roof and cantilever templates or any plane truss typed in, space trusses with ball-and-socket and link supports, method of joints and method of sections step by step, zero-force members, joint free-body diagrams, the largest load from member tension and compression limits, and the deflected truss with its animation); Centroids and Moments of Inertia (composite areas, wires and bodies, Pappus and Guldinus, principal axes and Mohr's circle; by integration from curves typed as formulas: the centroid and moments of inertia of an area, the centroid of a rod, the centroid and centre of mass of a solid of revolution with a density that may vary, and the resultant force and centre of pressure of a liquid on a submerged plate); Internal Forces (internal loadings at a cut, the six internal loadings at a section of a pipe assembly in space, shear and moment diagrams with the deflected shape and its animation, cables with point loads, uniform loads or their own weight, with supports at the same or different levels); Friction (blocks that slip or tip, a block sliding on an incline with static and kinetic friction or the μs at which motion impends, ladders, wedges, square-threaded screws, flat belts, ropes and V-belts over drums, band brakes on a wheel with the lever that tightens them; a spool, hoop or disk on a floor and against a wall turned in place; block brakes and pawls with self-locking; vehicles on two axles with each axle rolling freely, braked or driven, giving the axle reactions and friction when parked, the towing or pulling force that starts motion, and the steepest slope before slipping or tipping; collar, disk, pivot and journal bearings and rolling resistance; two bodies: blocks on an incline joined by a spring or cord, a block on a block with or without a cord to the wall, and whether a person can push or pull a crate); Virtual Work (any one-degree-of-freedom linkage typed in as point coordinates written as formulas in θ or x, with forces, weights, couples and springs, or a potential function given directly: every equilibrium position in a range with its stability from d²V/dθ², or the load that holds a given position; the mechanism is drawn at equilibrium with its virtual displacements and sweeps through its range on Play)
- **Dynamics of Rigid Bodies:** Rectilinear Kinematics (constant acceleration and free fall, two particles on the same line, s, v or a given as a function of time, a(v) and a(s), erratic motion from a v–t or a–t graph, with s–t, v–t and a–t graphs; dependent motion of blocks joined by cords over pulleys, with relative velocities and the time to reach a speed); Curvilinear Motion and Projectiles (projectiles on level ground, a slope or to a wall, found from the launch speed, the angle, the landing point or the flight time; normal and tangential components on a path y = f(x) or a circle; cylindrical components; relative motion of two particles); Particle Kinetics (a block on a floor or incline by ΣF = ma, impulse, work and energy or its stopping distance, with static and kinetic friction, a spring and the power used; two blocks joined by a cord over pulleys; a block launched by or landing on a spring; a car on a flat or banked curve; a particle on a vertical circle and where it leaves the track; stacked blocks and plates on a floor or incline, with a cord over a pulley joining the top and bottom ones, where every contact is checked for sticking or sliding and the force at which they first slip on each other is found; forces on a particle in cylindrical coordinates, with the path typed as r = f(θ) and z = h(θ) or as r(t), θ(t), z(t), split into F_r, F_θ, F_z, a push along a smooth guide, or a rotating arm and a smooth path or cam, with an optional spring; the power a motor needs to drive a vehicle or hoist up an incline against resistance, drag and a counterweight, or the steepest incline, top speed or acceleration for a given power; satellites and rockets under a central force, with the speeds at periapsis and apoapsis, the eccentricity, the path equation, the period, the circular and escape speeds, and the speed change to move from a circular orbit to another radius, all with free-body and kinetic diagrams); Impulse, Momentum and Impact (central impact with a measured or given coefficient of restitution and the energy lost, oblique impact, a ball off a wall or floor, and a bouncing ball; impulse from a force–time graph on a floor, incline or lift with friction checked before it moves; conservation of momentum when a spring pushes two bodies apart, one body throws another, or a block slides onto a cart; angular impulse from a moment M(t) and a particle pulled in on a cable; steady fluid streams through pipes and vanes with the support forces, the moment and a pin-and-roller split; variable mass for sand falling on a car, rockets and jet engines); Planar Kinematics of Rigid Bodies (gears and belt drives, slider-cranks, four-bar linkages, wheels and spools rolling without slipping, and a bar sliding on a wall and floor, by relative velocity and acceleration with the instantaneous centre marked; absolute motion of cams and linkages from a typed relation s = f(θ), either way between ω, α and v, a; rotating reference frames with every acceleration term and the Coriolis acceleration; slotted links driven by a peg on a disk or crank; planetary gears outside a sun gear or inside a ring gear); Planar Kinetics of Rigid Bodies (moments of inertia of composite bodies with holes; a car or motorcycle in translation with slipping wheels, locked brakes or a wheelie; a pendulum on a pin with its pin reactions; a drum lowering a block; a wheel or spool rolling on a floor, slope or fixed cord with the slip check, each by ΣF = ma_G, work and energy, or impulse and momentum where they apply, with the free-body diagram beside the kinetic diagram; two wheels joined by a link rolling down a slope, with the link force and a slip check; an arm driving gears round a fixed ring or sun gear by a couple; two bars released from rest, by conservation of energy; eccentric impact of a pendulum or hammer on a block or fixed stop with the rebound swing, a block tipping over a stop, and a disk or rod landing on a rough floor without slipping); Vibrations (a mass on springs in parallel or series, simple, compound and torsional pendulums, and energy-method systems such as a block on a cord over a disk or a cylinder rolling against a spring; viscous damping with several dampers side by side, and a bar on a pin held by a spring and a dashpot with the dashpot needed for critical damping; forcing by a periodic force, a moving support or a rotating unbalance, with the magnification factor and the response over time; a projectile embedding in the block with the largest compression; the time to come back to a given position; the differential equation and the equivalent L–R–C circuit); 3D Rigid-Body Motion (angular velocity and acceleration from rotations about carried axes, including bodies rolling without slipping, and the velocity and acceleration of a point, with relative motion along the body and a moving base; moments and products of inertia of composite bodies with the principal moments; angular momentum and kinetic energy; Euler's equations with the gyroscopic moment and the reactions at two shaft bearings; steady precession of tops and gyroscopes; torque-free motion of satellites and projectiles). Play runs each motion in a 10-second loop.
- **Mechanics of Deformable Bodies:** Simple Stress and Strain (normal stress, strain and Poisson's ratio in a bar; a weight hung on two rods; bolted and pinned joints checked for shear, bearing, net section and tear-out; sizing pins, rods, punched plates and collars from a factor of safety; reading E, yield, ultimate and fracture stress, resilience and toughness from stress-strain data, with unloading and the permanent set; a shear pad), with a material picker filled from the book's table of average properties; Axial Load (stepped bars with loads along them, free, fixed at both ends or short of a wall, with normal-force and displacement diagrams; members side by side or one inside another under a rigid cap; a rigid bar on rods or posts, with or without a pin; temperature change in all of these; stress concentration at a hole or shoulder fillets), solved in the book's order of equilibrium, compatibility and load-displacement; Torsion (stepped solid or tubular shafts, free or fixed at both ends, with torque and angle-of-twist diagrams and surface lines that twist; shafts sized from power and speed; a tube bonded to a core; square, triangular and elliptical shafts compared with a round shaft of the same area); Beam Stresses (bending stress by the flexure formula and shear stress by the shear formula on any section, a W shape from the table or a section built from parts, with the moment and shear taken from a beam or typed in; shear flow and fastener spacing in built-up beams; bending about an inclined axis with the tilted neutral axis; picking the lightest W shape or sizing a rectangle or round bar from the allowable stresses, then checking shear); Beam Deflection (slope and deflection anywhere on a beam with any pins, rollers and fixed supports, by integrating the elastic curve region by region with its boundary and continuity conditions, by adding the standard cases of Appendix D, or, for indeterminate beams such as a propped cantilever, a beam fixed at both ends or one continuous over a middle support, by removing the redundants and restoring compatibility; shear, moment, slope and deflection diagrams under an animated elastic curve); Combined Loadings (thin-walled cylinders and spheres: stresses, the largest pressure or the wall thickness, an axial force on the shell and the swelling; every stress component at a chosen point of a rectangular, round or tubular section carrying N, V, M and T together, with the section coloured by stress and the element at the point, which opens in Mohr's circle; a post loaded off centre with its corner stresses, neutral axis and kern); Stress and Strain Transformation (stress or strain on an element turned by any angle, principal values and their directions, the largest in-plane and absolute shear, on a Mohr's circle whose point x' can be dragged round to turn the element; 45°, 60° and general strain rosettes with the principal stresses from E and ν; generalized Hooke's law with the change in size, dilatation and bulk modulus); Buckling of Columns (Euler load and stress for pinned, fixed, fixed-pinned and fixed-free columns, with separate supports and bracing about each axis and the governing axis picked out; the yield check that tells when a column yields before it buckles; the allowable load with a factor of safety, the longest column for a load, and the smallest round or square bar or lightest W shape; animated buckled shapes with their effective lengths and the critical stress against slenderness chart)

Wherever a member deflects, Play bends it from straight to its deflected shape and back in a 10-second loop (speed 0.25× to 4×), in 2D and in 3D, coloured from blue (no deflection) to red (the largest). Every later module with a deflected shape follows the same rule.

Every diagram has a collapsible **Diagram options** bar under it that holds the switches which only change the picture (values on the diagram, the deflected shape, what Play animates, the truss tension/compression colours, the joint free-body diagram). Inputs keep only what changes the problem. Every later module puts its display switches in the same bar.

## Layout

- `index.html`: the shared header, the discipline tabs and the footer
- `shared/`: everything used by more than one discipline
  - `styles.css`, `ui.js` (controls and SVG drawing kit), `iso3d.js` (3D view), `charts.js`, `anim.js`
  - `core.js` (units, 4-significant-figure formatting, step recorder, preset merge), `app.js` (app shell), `report.js` (Print PDF)
  - `beam.js` (beam engine: reactions, N, V and M)
- `st/`: Statics solver. Each module has a physics file (`internal.js`) and a UI file (`modInternal.js`)
- `dy/`: Dynamics solver, laid out the same way, with `dycore.js` (units, polynomials, a Runge-Kutta integrator) and `dyui.js` (motion graphs, wheels, springs, the motion animation)
- `md/`: Mechanics of Deformable Bodies solver, laid out the same way, with `mdcore.js` (units, the material table, bar areas) and `mdui.js` (3D cylinders, arrows, the material picker)
- `vendor/`: html2canvas 1.4.1 and jsPDF 2.5.1 (MIT licensed), hosted here so the PDF feature needs no outside scripts
- `tests/`: `run.js` checks the physics against textbook answers; `presets.spec.js` loads every preset in a headless browser

Everything runs in the browser; there is no build step. To run it locally, serve the folder with any static server, for example `python3 -m http.server`, and open http://localhost:8000.
